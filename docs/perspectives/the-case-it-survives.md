# The case that it survives

> One side of the debate framed in [Will Scrum survive the AI era?](/perspectives/scrum-in-the-ai-era).
> This page argues the *yes* deliberately and one-sidedly. The opposing view is
> [the case that it fades](/perspectives/the-case-it-fades).

**Thesis: AI does not retire Scrum's kernel — it vindicates it.** Every force AI
introduces increases uncertainty about *correctness* and *intent*, and empirical process
control is the discipline for exactly that condition.

## Faster feedback is the whole point

Agile was a revolt against slow feedback. AI is a more extreme swing in the *same*
direction: prototype in minutes, not weeks. The premise — *inspect a real increment, then
adapt* — doesn't weaken when increments get cheaper; it gets more powerful. A team that
can run the loop ten times a day needs the loop's discipline more than the team that ran
it twice a month, not less.

## The bottleneck moved onto Scrum's home turf

When generation is free, the scarce resource is trust: *is this correct, and is it what
we meant?* That is not a coding question — it is an inspection question. The artifacts
Scrum already carries are the ones that answer it:

- **Definition of Ready / Definition of Done become more central, not less.** They are how
  a team states the bar that AI output must clear. Cheap generation makes the bar the
  product.
- **A named source of truth and preserved intent** are the only defence against
  confident, plausible, wrong work — which is the dominant failure mode of AI-assisted
  delivery.
- **Human accountability per artifact** stops being a platitude and becomes the load-bearing
  rule (see [the doctrine](/13-doctrine)). Someone owns the claim; someone verified it.

## The ceremonies that earn their keep

- **The retrospective survives on pure merit.** When tooling shifts monthly, *inspect and
  adapt of the human+AI system itself* is the highest-leverage hour in the building.
- **Review and demo become the heartbeat.** The synchronization point doesn't vanish — it
  relocates from "did we write it" to "did we verify it," which is where the risk now lives.
- **Intent alignment before work** matters more as the team gets smaller and the agents get
  faster — a wrong heading is now executed in an afternoon.

## Smaller teams still need the kernel

It is tempting to say "one human plus agents needs no process." The opposite holds: a small
team moving fast with powerful tools is *exactly* the team that drifts from intent without
noticing, accumulates unverified work, and mistakes motion for progress. The kernel —
transparency, inspection, adaptation — is the cheapest insurance against that drift, and it
costs less the smaller the team is.

## What this side concedes

This is a steelman, not the whole truth. It concedes that **the ceremony will change
shape** — story points and burndown are hard to defend — and that the *word* "Scrum" may
not survive even if its kernel does. But the discipline endures, because the problem it
solves got bigger, not smaller.

**Bottom line:** strip Scrum to transparency, inspection, adaptation, intent, and
verification, and AI makes that core *more* necessary. The kit you are reading is that core,
kept and sharpened for AI-heavy teams.
